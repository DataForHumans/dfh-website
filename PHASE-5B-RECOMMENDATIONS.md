# Phase 5B creative direction

## Owner direction

Connectedness is the organizing principle for Data for Humans. The owner-approved [manifesto](BRAND-MANIFESTO.md) guides copy, composition, photography, network artwork, and information design.

The owner selected Connected composition, developed from Concept B. The homepage opens with “We see the world in connections” and concrete data, research, and AI consulting copy. The hero network crosses from cream into the sage credibility band. Forest green gives the editorial feature its own space. Use Data for Humans alone as the closing signature.

## Positioning

Data for Humans helps organizations do work that matters to people. It also takes care with how that work is recorded, understood and communicated. Records reflect what people had time, reason and means to collect. Frontline staff can explain circumstances that the records miss. Analysis should bring those perspectives together, make the evidence understandable and leave clients able to make their own decisions and maintain their own systems.

Show compassion through listening and privacy, competence through usable tools and specific evidence, and humility through plain explanations of what the evidence supports. Give these practices prominence in the places they already appear.

## What the references contribute

- [Namaste Data](https://www.namastedata.org/) connects purpose to the practice of listening, learning and deciding how to use data and AI. On About, use the existing working principles to show how staff knowledge informs interpretation.
- [Data+Soul](https://www.dataplussoul.com/) makes collaborative inquiry visible through its evaluation and facilitation work. Show a working session where people are making sense of something together.
- [3iap](https://3iap.com/) puts finished analytical work and the questions behind it on display. Keep the authentic Work exhibits prominent and readable.
- [We All Count](https://weallcount.com/) focuses on choices made throughout an analysis. Show concrete choices Data for Humans already makes, such as agreeing what partners need to see before sharing records.
- [data.org](https://data.org/) connects capability building with practical use. Bring the Connection Map handoff and the client’s freedom to choose an implementation partner closer to the exhibit.
- [Giorgia Lupi’s Data Humanism manifesto](https://giorgialupi.com/data-humanism-my-manifesto-for-a-new-data-world) treats records as partial views of reality. Keep source, scope, and limits close to the evidence they qualify.
- [Dear Data](https://www.dear-data.com/theproject) shows how observation and context turn a record into something another person can understand. Look for gestures of attention and interpretation in photography.
- [Urban Institute’s Do No Harm Project](https://www.urban.org/projects/do-no-harm-project) examines how presentation can misrepresent people. Review who is visible in each exhibit, what a reader might infer, and which details must stay private.

## Page and art direction

- Keep the hero, metrics, authentic project evidence, forest-green editorial feature, Connection Map, event when scheduled, closing invitation, and footer in that order. Keep the philosophical statement in an italic serif and the consulting description and booking actions close by. “No one sees the whole picture alone” connects the feature to the About manifesto.
- About gives the complete approved manifesto an editorial presentation. Keep its wording, order, punctuation and closing signature. Keep team portraits and the existing practical working principles.
- Services should give the accessible Connection Map animation and ownership terms editorial weight in their existing section. Work should let the dashboard and chart carry the evidence, with plain annotations, enough space to read them, and clear disclosures. Preserve analytical data, relationships, approved interpretations and disclosures. Keep the entire approved Crowded Closet case study verbatim.
- Treat the network as a visual metaphor for people and the relationships that let them work together. Give paths slightly more weight and use a solid ochre node without a ring. Use ochre to draw attention to one relationship or to a node near the edge of a group. Distribute branches across the network to show interdependence. Use deliberate curves, a mix of outlined and filled nodes, clear negative space, and paths that visibly leave the frame. Keep fading, particles, human icons and analytical labels out of the artwork.
- The homepage feature uses a complete forest-green composition with decorative network artwork until a photograph is approved. Keep generated review imagery in local documentation. Preserve the existing color team portrait assets.

## Photography to commission

| Priority and placement | Subject | Orientation, crop, and source size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary, homepage forest-green feature | A real working session with two or three people listening and making sense of information together. Look for one person explaining and another responding, with a worksheet or an exchange of papers providing context. | Horizontal 3:2 or 4:3 original, at least 2400 × 1600 px after any initial crop. Compose the gesture and the people involved in the central two-thirds. Leave enough space for a 3:2 desktop crop and a 4:3 mobile crop. |
| Alternate, same placement | A closer view of two people working through a blank or approved anonymized worksheet. Keep the exchange legible in the image, such as a hand pointing and another taking notes. | Horizontal 3:2, at least 2400 × 1600 px. Keep the gesture and sheet within the central 60% of the frame. |
| Optional future editorial use, subject to review | Staff or volunteers exchanging supplies or working side by side. Choose an image where cooperation is the subject. | Horizontal 4:3 or 3:2, at least 2000 × 1334 px. Include enough context for a horizontal crop and retain the original. |

Supply the original color file and a neutral black-and-white reference if available. Confirm permission from identifiable people and the organization. Check paperwork and screens for identifying details. The owner approves the photo, crop, and caption before publication.
